# Contributing to ViD

ViD / VisionDelivery.ai is maintained, operated and published by [AI Value Link](https://aivaluelink.com). The official project website is [VisionDelivery.ai](https://visiondelivery.ai), and contributions are coordinated through [VisionDeliveryAi/vid](https://github.com/VisionDeliveryAi/vid).

We welcome contributions of code, prompts, workflows, tests and documentation.

## Authority and provenance

Submit only material that you have the right to contribute under these terms. Obtain employer, client or other rights-holder permission where necessary. Identify incorporated third-party material, its source, its licence and any notices that must be retained. Do not present someone else's work as your own or remove required notices.

Do not submit credentials, private customer material, personal data or other confidential information. Customer-specific deliverables must remain separate from reusable project contributions unless the necessary permissions have been obtained.

## Sign off public contributions

For contributions to the public project, sign off each commit to confirm that you can truthfully make the certifications in the [Developer Certificate of Origin 1.1](DCO). Use your name and an email address you are authorised to make public:

```text
Signed-off-by: Your Name <your-email@example.com>
```

Git can add this line using:

```sh
git commit -s
```

The DCO records contribution authority and provenance. The contribution licence above supplies the Apache 2.0 permissions; a sign-off does not assign copyright. The DCO also explains that the contribution and sign-off record are public and may be retained and redistributed.

If the project or contribution is private and the DCO's public-contribution certification is not accurate, contact the maintainers for a written Apache 2.0 contribution and provenance confirmation instead. Do not make an inaccurate DCO certification.

## AI-assisted work

AI-assisted contributions are welcome under the same contribution terms. Review the submitted material, understand its behaviour and take responsibility for its suitability. Identify known third-party sources, copied material and applicable restrictions. Do not submit AI output that you know you are not authorised to contribute.

Describe material AI assistance in your pull request, including the tool used and the human review or verification performed. You do not need to publish private prompts or conversation logs. AI assistance does not itself establish copyright ownership or clear third-party rights.

## Propose and verify a change

1. Check existing issues and pull requests for related work. Discuss substantial changes with the maintainers before investing in a large implementation.
2. Follow the repository's development instructions and applicable project conventions. Keep changes focused and include relevant documentation.
3. Run the checks appropriate to the change using the repository's documented process. Add or update meaningful tests when behaviour changes.
4. In the pull request, explain the problem, the resulting behaviour and the verification performed. Identify third-party material and material AI assistance.
5. Include the required public DCO sign-offs or the agreed private contribution confirmation.

Maintainers review contributions for project fit, quality, licensing and provenance. Submission does not guarantee acceptance. Contribution alone does not appoint you as an official maintainer, operator or branded commercial provider.

Report vulnerabilities using [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), rather than a public issue.
