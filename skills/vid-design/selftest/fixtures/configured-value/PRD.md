## Seed

I want a plain link shortener for our newsletter team: paste a long
address, get a short one that redirects, no account needed. Links should
stop working after a while so the service never becomes a permanent
open redirect, and the person who made a link must be able to remove it.
It has to be hard to abuse — no scripts flooding it, no script-scheme
targets. Nothing else: no custom words in the short address, no editing,
no dashboards.

## Thesis

### Product summary

Shortlink turns a long web address into a short redirecting link in one
step, with no account. Every link expires at a known time — thirty days
at most — and its creator can remove it earlier from a private page
reached only through a deletion link issued once, on the receipt shown
right after creation. The public intake is bounded per source address
and accepts only web targets; an operator can stop any link, or every link to an exact host name, from a credential-protected console that shows what an act would affect before it is confirmed.

### Primary users

Newsletter editors and event organisers who paste addresses into places
where length is costly (mail, chat, a printed weekly programme) and who
need the link to work for the weeks the item is current. One operator —
a member of the same team — handles abuse complaints and keeps the
service running.

### MVP focus

- G1: A long address becomes a short, stable redirecting link in one step, without an account.
- G2: Every link stops working at a known time and its creator can remove it earlier by a deliberate act.
- G3: The public intake is bounded per source and service-wide, accepts only web targets, and every link — or every listed link to an exact host name — can be stopped by the operator after seeing what the act affects.

### Explicitly not MVP

Accounts of any kind; custom aliases; editing a link's target; analytics
beyond the click count shown on a link's own private page; a
visitor-facing abuse-report form (abuse reaches the support address, and
an operator disables the link); recovery of a lost deletion link (the receipt is shown once and says so — a creator who loses it waits for expiry; the operator cannot verify who made a link and never disables on a creator's say-so); QR codes; more than one language or time
zone (English, times in UTC); a watch on the short domain's standing with
mail and browser blocklists (the intake is open to strangers by the seed's
"no account needed", and the pilot accepts the reputation risk that
carries, A-014); an and-its-subdomains host act on the console (the exact
host name was chosen so a shared platform is never taken down for every
editor; the wildcard-subdomain campaign shape is the accepted cost,
A-005). Each is deferred by the owner (A-002, A-005, A-007, A-009,
A-014), not forgotten.

### Required core flows

- An editor pastes an address, receives the once-shown receipt with the short link, its expiry time and the private deletion link, keeps the deletion link, and shares the short link.
- A reader follows the short link and lands on the target; after expiry, removal or disabling the reader sees a page saying so and nothing else.
- A creator opens the deletion link, sees the link's click count, and removes the link by one deliberate act.
- An operator looks a reported code or host up on the console, sees its state and target (or the host's active links listed with their targets), and disables it — by its code, or every listed link to that exact host name, or a chosen subset — with a categorised reason; the disable is recorded, a link disable is final, a host block can be lifted.

### Value logic

Editors return because a link made here is short, works for the weeks
the item is current, and can be withdrawn on purpose and never by
accident; the operator keeps running it because it cannot become a permanent open redirect, its intake is bounded, and an abusive host can be shut in one confirmed act that shows its reach first. A link that outlives its purpose, a link that cannot
be withdrawn, or a link withdrawn by nobody's intent is the failure that
ends both.

### Evidence basis

The seed is the only source in this run; no conflicts. The seed's "after
a while" became thirty days by owner answer (A-001); "remove it" became
the private page with one Remove control (A-002); "hard to abuse"
became the per-address bound with its IPv6 rule and the operator's
disable-by-host (A-004, A-005); the proof metric excludes the delivery-time burst of fetches by owner answer (A-006).

## Actors and journeys

### Editor

1. Drafts the weekly newsletter with three long addresses in it (off_screen)
2. Pastes each address on the create page, copies the short link from the once-shown receipt, and keeps the receipt's deletion link in the team's notes
3. Sends the newsletter carrying the short links (off_screen)
4. A week later, one item is withdrawn: opens that link's deletion link from the notes, sees its click count, and confirms Remove

### Reader

1. Reads the newsletter in a mail client (off_screen)
2. Follows a short link and lands on the target
3. Follows the withdrawn item's link and sees the "removed by its creator" page, which names no target

### Operator

1. Receives an abuse complaint at the support address (off_screen)
2. Opens the operator console, enters the reported code and sees its state and target without following it, then disables it — or enters the exact host name, sees the active links that point there with their targets, and disables them all or a chosen subset — choosing a reason category
3. Confirms the code now answers the "disabled" page, which names no target

```vid-prd-mechanisms

MECH-03 | G3 | Creation is bounded per source address (an IPv6 address counted by its /64 prefix) and accepts only http or https targets; an operator can look a code or an exact host name up on a credential-protected console, see its state and target — or the host's active links listed with their targets — and disable it, all listed links or a chosen subset; a disabled host refuses new links for thirty days unless the operator lifts the block; a link disable is final. | stated: seed "no scripts flooding it, no script-scheme targets"
```
